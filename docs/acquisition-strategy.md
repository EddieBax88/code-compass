#### Template 1: The "15-Second NEC Hunting Protocol" (For `r/ElectricalApprentices` & JATC Groups)

> **Title**: Stop trying to memorize 1,200 pages of the NEC. Here is the 15-second "hunting protocol" to pass your exam.
>
> **Body**:
> Hey guys,
>
> One of the biggest mistakes I see apprentices make when prepping for the Journeyman exam is trying to *memorize* rules. The exam isn't a memory contest — it's a **book navigation race**. You have ~2.4 minutes per question.
>
> Here is the exact 4-step hunting protocol to locate any article in under 60 seconds:
>
> 1. **Classify the Subject (Codeology)**: Is this General (Ch. 1-4), Specific Occupancies (Ch. 5), Equipment (Ch. 6), or Special Conditions (Ch. 7)?
> 2. **Identify the Index Keyword**: Never look up the answer directly in the index. Look up the *noun* or *equipment type* (e.g., for "wall outlet spacing in a bedroom", search `Receptacle Outlets`, NOT `Bedroom`).
> 3. **Trace the Drill-Down Path**: `Receptacle Outlets` → `Dwelling Units` → `General Provisions` → **210.52(A)**.
> 4. **Locate & Verify in the Book**: Turn to 210.52(A), check for exceptions, and confirm the exact distance (12 ft rule / 6 ft from any point).
>
> We built a free tool called **Code Compass** ([codecompass.work](https://codecompass.work)) that trains this exact hunting protocol — you paste any scenario or question, and it gives you the exact index drill-down trail to trace in your physical codebook.
>
> Give the 15-second drill a try on your next practice question and let me know if it speeds up your lookup time!

#### Template 2: Solving Tricky Dwelling & Box Fill Calculations (For Facebook Groups & ElectricianTalk)

> **Title**: Step-by-Step Breakdown: How to calculate Box Fill without getting tripped up by Table 314.16(B)
>
> **Body**:
> Quick review for anyone prepping for their test or wiring boxes in the field:
>
> Box fill questions trip up a lot of apprentices because of the volume allowance rules in **NEC 314.16(B)**. Here is the quick cheat sheet:
>
> - **Conductors**: 1 volume allowance per conductor originating outside the box. (Table 314.16(B) volume based on conductor size: 14 AWG = 2.00 in³, 12 AWG = 2.25 in³, 10 AWG = 2.50 in³).
> - **Internal Clamps**: 1 volume allowance TOTAL (based on the largest conductor connected to the clamp), no matter how many clamps are in the box.
> - **Support Fittings (Hickeys/Studs)**: 1 volume allowance TOTAL based on the largest conductor.
> - **Devices (Switches/Receptacles)**: **2 volume allowances** based on the largest conductor connected to that device.
> - **Equipment Grounding Conductors (EGC)**: 1 volume allowance total for up to 4 EGCs (based on the largest EGC). *Note: 2020/2023 NEC adds 1/4 volume for each additional EGC beyond 4.*
>
> **Example**:
> Box with (4) 12 AWG wires, (1) duplex receptacle, internal cable clamps, and (2) grounding conductors:
> - 4 × 12 AWG wires = 4 × 2.25 = **9.00 in³**
> - Clamps = 1 × 2.25 = **2.25 in³**
> - Receptacle (Double volume) = 2 × 2.25 = **4.50 in³**
> - Grounding Wires = 1 × 2.25 = **2.25 in³**
> - **Total Volume Required**: **18.00 in³**
>
> If you want an instant way to double-check your box fill, conduit fill, or motor calculations on your phone on the job site, check out the free calculator engine at **[codecompass.work](https://codecompass.work)**.
>
> Stay safe out there!
